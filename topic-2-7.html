2.7 VSEPR, Shapes & Polarity

VSEPR: electron domains (each bond — single, double or triple — counts as one; each lone pair counts as one; a lone electron counts too) repel each other and spread as far apart as possible. Electron-domain geometry comes from total domains; molecular geometry describes only the atoms' positions.

  • Lone pairs take more space than bonding pairs (only attracted by one nucleus, so spread out): they squeeze bond angles. Compare CH₄ 109.5° > NH₃ ~107° > H₂O ~104.5°: same 4 domains, more lone pairs → smaller angle. Multiple bonds also push neighbors slightly (BF₃ 120° vs. O₃ 117° due to the lone pair).
  • Lone pair placement in trigonal bipyramid: lone pairs go in equatorial positions (more room), giving seesaw, T-shape, linear. In an octahedron, two lone pairs go opposite each other → square planar.

Molecular polarity

  1. Draw the correct Lewis structure and shape.
  2. Mark each bond as polar/nonpolar (ΔEN) and draw the dipole arrows.
  3. Do the vector sum. If bond dipoles cancel by symmetry → nonpolar (dipole moment = 0). Otherwise → polar.
Shortcut: symmetric shapes with identical terminal atoms and no lone pairs on the center (linear, trigonal planar, tetrahedral, trigonal bipyramidal, octahedral) → nonpolar. Add a lone pair or replace one terminal atom with a different one and symmetry breaks → polar. Exceptions to "lone pair = polar": lone pairs that are symmetrically placed (linear XeF₂-type, square planar XeF₄-type) cancel. Examples: CCl₄ nonpolar, CHCl₃ polar, PCl₃ polar, SF₆ nonpolar, SF₅Cl polar, BrF₃ polar, ICl₄⁻ nonpolar. A linear molecule can be polar if the two terminal atoms differ (HCN-type, OCS).

Why bother? Polar molecules have stronger IMFs, higher boiling points, and align in an electric field (the PhET plates idea); nonpolar molecules show no alignment.

Hybridization, σ and π Bonds

  • Count domains on the atom → hybridization: 2 = sp (180°), 3 = sp² (120°), 4 = sp³ (109.5°). Lone pairs count! NH₃ N is sp³; water O is sp³; the carbon in a C=O is sp².
  • Shortcut for carbon: 4 single bonds → sp³; one double bond → sp²; one triple (or two doubles) → sp. Hydrogenating a triple bond to a double, or a double to a single, steps the carbon sp → sp² → sp³.
  • σ bond: head-on (end-to-end) overlap; density between nuclei; every bond has exactly one σ. π bond: side-by-side p overlap above/below the axis. Single = 1σ; double = 1σ + 1π; triple = 1σ + 2π.
  • σ overlap is stronger than π, so the first bond of a double bond is stronger than the second; this is why C=C (614) is less than twice C–C (348 → ~696).
  • π bonds prevent rotation around the bond axis; this makes cis/trans (geometric) isomers possible (e.g. 2-butene, 1,2-dichloroethene). Single bonds rotate freely.
  • Count σ and π: count every line between atoms for σ (each bond = 1), then add the extra lines from double (1 π) and triple (2 π) bonds. Lone pairs are not bonds.
Worked count (propene, CH₃–CH=CH₂): σ count = 6 C–H + 1 C–C single + 1 (the σ part of C=C) = 8 σ; the second line of C=C is the 1 π. Carbons: CH₃ sp³; the two alkene carbons sp².

Practice Problems

Try each before opening the answer.

1. Give the shape, polarity, and hybridization: SiF₄, PH₃, ICl₂⁻, BrF₃.
SiF₄: tetrahedral, nonpolar, sp³. PH₃: trigonal pyramidal, polar, sp³. ICl₂⁻: linear, nonpolar (3 lone pairs). BrF₃: T-shaped, polar.
2. Rank the H–X–H angle in NH₄⁺, NH₃, H₂O.
NH₄⁺ (109.5°) > NH₃ (~107°) > H₂O (~104.5°). More lone pairs compress the angle.
3. Count σ and π bonds and give hybridization of each C and N in CH₂=CH–C≡N.
6 σ, 3 π. CH₂ and CH carbons sp²; nitrile C and N sp.
4. Why does 1,2-dichloroethene have geometric isomers but 1,2-dichloroethane does not?
The C=C π bond blocks rotation, locking the Cl positions. A C–C single bond rotates freely.
5. A molecule has the formula AX₂ and is linear. Give two ways it could be polar or nonpolar.
Nonpolar if the two A–X dipoles are equal and opposite. Polar if the end atoms differ (e.g. OCS-type), so dipoles do not cancel.

FRQ Practice

Write a full response first, then check the scoring points.

(a) Draw NH₂Cl and give its shape and bond angle. (b) Is it polar? (c) For CH₃CN, count σ and π bonds and give the hybridization of each carbon.
(a) 4 domains (3 bonds, 1 lp): trigonal pyramidal, ~107° (<109.5°). (b) Polar (pyramidal, unequal bonds). (c) σ: 3 C–H + 1 C–C + 1 (σ of C≡N) = 5 σ; π: 2. CH₃ carbon sp³; nitrile carbon sp.
