2.6 Resonance & Formal Charge

Resonance

  • When same atom connectivity can be drawn several equally valid ways (differ only in electron placement), the real structure is a hybrid; electrons are delocalized. Draw all contributors with a double-headed arrow ↔.
  • The structures do not flip back and forth — the molecule is always the average.
  • Evidence: measured bond lengths are equal and between the single and double values. Bond order = (total bonds among the atoms)/(number of equivalent positions). O₃: 1.5 (128 pm, between O–O 148 and O=O 121). NO₃⁻: N–O order 1⅓. SO₃²⁻, CO₃²⁻, etc. follow the same math.

Formal charge

FC = (valence e⁻) − (nonbonding e⁻) − (number of bonds). Sum of FCs = overall charge.

Choosing the best structure: (1) formal charges closest to zero; (2) any negative FC on the more electronegative atom; (3) avoid adjacent like charges; (4) octet rule satisfied where possible. Formal charge is bookkeeping, not real charge.

Worked example — SO₄²⁻

Structure A (all single bonds): S: 6 − 0 − 4 = +2; each O: 6 − 6 − 1 = −1. Structure B (two S=O, two S–O⁻): S: 6 − 0 − 6 = 0; double-bonded O: 6 − 4 − 2 = 0; single-bonded O: −1. B has smaller formal charges, so it's preferred in many courses (for AP, either may be accepted; know why B is "better" by FC). Resonance among the double-bond placements applies.

Worked example — comparing structures

For a molecule written two ways, calculate FC on each atom of each. The better structure is the one with zeros (or the −1 on O/N/F rather than on C/S). If one candidate puts +1 on the most electronegative atom (O or N), reject it.

Common errors: forgetting to add/subtract for ion charge; putting H or F in the middle; giving H a lone pair; a double bond to F or Cl (F never); drawing a resonance form that moves atoms (only electrons move).

Practice Problems

Try each before opening the answer.

1. Assign formal charges: O=C=S versus O≡C–S⁻ (S has 3 lone pairs). Which is better?
O=C=S: all zeros. Other: O +1, C 0, S −1. O=C=S is better (zeros; no +1 on the most electronegative atom).
2. Benzene has two Kekulé structures. What is the C–C bond order and why?
Each C–C alternates single/double between structures: average 1.5. Real bonds are all equal in length.
3. Find formal charges in PO₄³⁻ with all single bonds, then with one P=O.
All single: P +1, each O −1 (sum −3). With one P=O: P 0, double O 0, three O −1. The second has smaller formal charges.
4. Why is BeF₂ drawn with single bonds rather than Be=F?
Double bonds would give Be −2 and F +1, a bad charge distribution, so an incomplete octet on Be is preferred.
5. NO has 11 valence electrons. What does this show about the Lewis model?
It can't give every atom an octet; the Lewis model has limits with odd-electron species.

FRQ Practice

Write a full response first, then check the scoring points.

Isocyanic acid, HNCO, can be drawn as (1) H–N=C=O or (2) H–N–C≡O (N with 2 lone pairs, O with 1). (a) Assign formal charges. (b) Which structure is preferred and why? (c) In NO₃⁻, what is the N–O bond order?
(a) (1) all 0. (2) N −1, C 0, O +1. (b) Structure 1: zeros; (2) puts +1 on the most electronegative atom. (c) 4 bonds shared among 3 positions → 1⅓.
