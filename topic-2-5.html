Valence counting, skeletons, octet exceptions

2.5 Lewis Diagrams

Valence electrons for main-group elements = group number (1,2, then 13→3 … 18→8). The octet rule: atoms (C, N, O, F especially) tend to be surrounded by 8 valence electrons; H gets 2. Typical bonding capacity: H 1, F/Cl 1, O 2, N 3, C 4.

Procedure

  1. Count total valence electrons. Add for every atom; for anions add electrons equal to the charge; for cations subtract.
  2. Skeleton: the unique/least-electronegative atom is central (never H or F); connect with single bonds. Polyatomic ions go in brackets with the charge at top right.
  3. Fill terminal atoms' octets with lone pairs (H gets none).
  4. Place leftover electrons on the central atom.
  5. If the central atom lacks an octet, convert terminal lone pairs into double/triple bonds.
  6. Check: total electrons used = step 1 total; formal charges are small.

Worked example 1 — PCl₃

Valence e⁻: 5 + 3(7) = 26. Skeleton: P center, three P–Cl bonds (6 e⁻). Each Cl gets 3 lone pairs (18 e⁻) → 24 used. Leftover 2 e⁻ = one lone pair on P. P: 3 bonds + 1 lone pair = octet. ✔ (3 bonding + 1 lone pair → 4 domains.)

Worked example 2 — ClO₃⁻

7 + 3(6) + 1 = 26. Cl center, three single bonds (6), each O gets 3 lone pairs (18) = 24; the remaining 2 e⁻ go on Cl as a lone pair. Every atom has an octet. Formal charge on Cl = 7 − 2 − 3 = +2, which is large; converting some Cl–O to Cl=O (Period 3 may expand its octet) lowers it — see 2.6.

Worked example 3 — hydrocarbon rule

For hydrocarbons (CₙHₘ): each C needs 4 bonds. C₃H₈ is all single bonds; C₃H₆ (no ring) needs one C=C. Count "degrees of unsaturation": each C=C or ring removes two H from CₙH₂ₙ₊₂.

Exceptions to the octet rule

  • Incomplete octet: Be (4 e⁻ around Be in BeX₂), B (6 e⁻ in BX₃). Usually drawn without forcing double bonds to F because that yields bad formal charges.
  • Expanded octet (hypervalent): Period 3 or lower central atoms (P, S, Cl, Br, I, Xe) can hold 10 or 12 electrons: PCl₅, SF₆, ICl₄⁻, XeF₂. Period 2 cannot (too small, no NF₅ or OF₆).
  • Odd-electron species: NO, NO₂ — can't give every atom an octet. A limitation of the Lewis model.

Hypervalent trick

Place all leftover pairs on the central atom after octets on terminals. Example IF₅: 7 + 35 = 42 e⁻; 5 I–F bonds (10) + 15 lone pairs on F (30) = 40; 2 left → 1 lone pair on I → 6 domains, 5 bonding + 1 lone.

Practice Problems

Try each before opening the answer.

1. HOCl: total valence e⁻ and structure.
1 + 6 + 7 = 14. H–O–Cl (O central); O has 2 lone pairs, Cl has 3.
2. N₂H₄: total e⁻ and structure.
10 + 4 = 14. H₂N–NH₂, one lone pair on each N.
3. CH₃OH: total e⁻ and structure.
4 + 4 + 6 = 14. C bonded to three H and O; O bonded to H and C with 2 lone pairs.
4. ClO₂⁻: total e⁻ and structure.
7 + 12 + 1 = 20. O–Cl–O with single bonds; each O 3 lone pairs; Cl 2 lone pairs (bent).
5. ICl₄⁻: total e⁻ and why the octet rule is exceeded.
7 + 28 + 1 = 36. Four I–Cl bonds, 3 lone pairs on each Cl, 2 lone pairs on I (12 e⁻ around I). I is Period 5 so it can expand its octet.
6. Why can't NF₅ exist while PF₅ does?
N is Period 2, too small to hold 10 electrons; P is Period 3 and can expand.

FRQ Practice

Write a full response first, then check the scoring points.

Cl₂O has the skeleton Cl O Cl. (a) Complete the Lewis diagram. (b) State the electron-domain count and hybridization of O. (c) Predict whether the Cl–O–Cl angle is greater or less than 109.5° and justify.
(a) 7+6+7 = 20 e⁻: two O–Cl bonds, O has 2 lone pairs, each Cl 3 lone pairs. (b) 4 domains → sp³. (c) Less than 109.5° (VSEPR argument): O has two lone pairs, which occupy more space and repel the bonding pairs more strongly, so the molecule is bent.
