2.4 Metals and Alloys

  • Sea-of-electrons model: positive metal cores (nucleus + inner electrons) in a lattice, surrounded by delocalized valence electrons not tied to any one atom.
  • Conductivity: mobile electrons carry charge in the solid and liquid state; the same mobility conducts heat. Malleable/ductile: layers slide, cores stay surrounded by the electron sea, and bonding is maintained (no repulsion crisis like ionic). Luster also comes from mobile electrons.
  • Alloy = mixture of metal with other element(s).
    SubstitutionalInterstitial
    Atom sizescomparable radiione much smaller (usually a nonmetal)

    Examplesbrass (Cu + Zn), white gold (Au + Pd), sterling silver (Ag + Cu)steel (Fe + C)
    Effectmixes propertieslayers can't slide easily → harder, stronger, less malleable
  • Steel carbon content: more interstitial C → more rigid/hard, more brittle (low-carbon: malleable; high-carbon: hard, cutting tools).
  • Size test: compare radii in a table. Within ~10–15 % of each other → substitutional. A big gap (metal vs. small nonmetal like B, C, N, H) → interstitial. Draw interstitial atoms small, in gaps; substitutional atoms same size, in lattice positions.

Practice Problems

Try each before opening the answer.

1. Classify as substitutional or interstitial: bronze (Cu 128 pm, Sn 140 pm), Fe + N, Pd + H.
Bronze: substitutional (similar radii). Fe + N and Pd + H: interstitial (small nonmetal atom fits in the holes).
2. Why can a metal conduct electricity as a solid but an ionic solid cannot?
A metal has delocalized valence electrons free to move; an ionic solid's ions are locked in place.
3. Why is copper malleable but CaF₂ brittle?
Cu layers slide while the electron sea keeps bonding intact. In CaF₂ sliding aligns like charges, which repel and fracture the crystal.
4. Why does adding carbon to iron make it harder?
Small C atoms in the interstitial holes block layers from sliding past each other.
5. Draw (describe) the particle diagram for an alloy with Au and Pd that are close in size.
A single ordered lattice of similar-size circles, with some Au positions replaced by Pd (substitutional); no small atoms in gaps.

FRQ Practice

Write a full response first, then check the scoring points.

Brass (Cu, Zn) and steel (Fe, C). (a) Classify each alloy and justify using atomic size. (b) Explain why both conduct electricity. (c) Explain why steel with more carbon is harder. (d) Describe the particle diagram for each.
(a) Brass substitutional (Cu 128 pm, Zn 134 pm, similar); steel interstitial (C much smaller than Fe). (b) Delocalized valence electrons are mobile. (c) More C atoms in holes block sliding of layers. (d) Brass: a lattice where some Cu atoms are replaced by same-size Zn. Steel: lattice of Fe with small C atoms in the gaps.
