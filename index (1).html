Writing Free-Response Answers

Template — polarity/EN: "Cl is in the same period as P but farther right, so Cl has greater nuclear charge (more protons, same shell) and a higher electronegativity. The shared electrons are drawn toward Cl, so Cl is δ− and P is δ+."
Template — bond length/energy: "Br has more electron shells than Cl, so its atomic radius is larger and the Br–Br nuclei sit farther apart; a longer bond is weaker, so it has a smaller bond energy."
Template — lattice energy: "By Coulomb's law, the attraction is proportional to q₁q₂/r². Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ have larger charges (and smaller radii) than Na⁺ and F⁻, so the attraction is stronger and the lattice energy is greater."
Template — bond angle: "Both have four electron domains, but NH₃ has a lone pair, which occupies more space and repels bonding pairs more strongly, compressing the angle below 109.5°."
Template — formal charge: "Structure 2 has formal charges of zero on every atom, while structure 1 has +1 on a more electronegative atom, so structure 2 is the better representation."
  • Always name the periodic trend AND the reason (shells, nuclear charge, radius) when a question says "justify."
  • When asked "less than or greater than," pick, then give the cause-and-effect chain.
  • Particle diagram "identify an error and fix it": name one specific error (charge, ratio, relative size, arrangement), then say exactly what the corrected drawing shows.
  • Lewis diagrams: show all lone pairs, charges in brackets, and recheck your electron count.
  • Don't confuse intramolecular (bonds) with intermolecular (between molecules) when explaining melting points: molecules melt by overcoming IMFs; ionic solids by overcoming ionic bonds.

One-glance checklist

EN trend → ΔEN → bond type → dipole direction → Lewis → domains → shape → symmetry → molecular polarity → hybridization → σ/π. And for strength: charge, size, bond order.

Mixed Review

1. Justify: Br–Br has a smaller bond energy than Cl–Cl.
Br has more shells and a larger radius, so the bond is longer; a longer bond is weaker.
2. Explain why SF₆ is nonpolar and SF₄ is polar.
SF₆ octahedral: dipoles cancel. SF₄ seesaw (a lone pair): dipoles do not cancel.
3. Predict whether the C–C bond energy in C₂H₄ is greater or less than in C₂H₆ and justify.
Greater: C=C bond order 2 is shorter and stronger than C–C.
4. Predict the type of bonding in MgBr₂ and give two supporting properties.
Ionic: high melting point; conducts when molten/dissolved.
5. Identify one error in a LiBr diagram with Li⁺ larger than Br⁻.
Li⁺ is smaller than Br⁻; draw the cation smaller.
